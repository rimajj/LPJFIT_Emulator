### Added

- **`scripts/diag_level_binding.py` — which of the 22 scored quantities actually holds the level model's score down, and which of its errors are reducible at all.** Read-only over the out-of-fold predictions the level-map job already wrote, so it fits nothing, needs no SLURM and no experiment id, and produces no new skill number: it decomposes an already-reported one. Two things it computes that a per-quantity pass rate cannot. First, the **leave-one-out oracle** — replace one quantity by the truth and recompute the conjunctive statistic — because the blessed statistic is conjunctive, so a quantity's marginal worth is not its own pass rate. Second, a split of every quantity's error by **whether LPJmL-FIT reproduces itself**: the acceptance band is `max(10 %, the two-seed relative spread)`, so the stored band recovers that spread wherever it exceeds the 10 % floor and only there, which separates emulator error that is reducible from realisation scatter nobody can predict. Every oracle number in its output has seen the answer for the quantity it substitutes and is labelled as a bound, never as skill.

### Changed

- **The next lever on the level model is rooting depth and biomass, not the height tail — and that reverses the previous record's priority without retracting any of its numbers.** Perfecting the predicted 90th-percentile height outright moves the conjunctive score from 0.0361 to 0.0367, `+0.0006`, which is 37 cells of 56,950; only 37 cells fail it alone. Rooting depth's three quantiles are worth `+0.0419` between them — as much as all four carbon and count stocks together, from three quantities instead of four, and seventy times the height tail. Above-ground biomass is worth `+0.0097` and soil carbon `+0.0090`. Basis: the level model's own out-of-fold predictions over 56,950 cells, LPJmL-FIT historical leg, state at 1999, climate window 1970–1999, npatch=25, truth = mean of seeds 1+2, blocked spatial folds k=5 at 15°; the geographic-address null scores 0.0187 on the same statistic.
- **Biomass is the height-tail error carried through the allometry, which is how both records are true at once.** The measured elasticity is `d log(agb) / d log(height_p90) = +2.885`, and 2.885 × the 6.3 % median height-tail error is 18.1 % against the 18.7 % median biomass error actually observed. So the height tail keeps its place in the queue but acquires a target instead of an adjective: it must come under **3.5 %** median error, because that is what puts biomass inside a 10 % band. It also follows that the directly predicted biomass carries no information the height heads do not already have — the two log errors correlate `+0.776` — so imposing the predicted biomass on the roster cannot help.
- **Failure is a property of the cell, not of the quantity.** The conjunctive score is 0.0361 against the 0.0002 expected if the 22 failures were independent, a 151-fold clustering: 3.6 % of cells fail nothing, 23.4 % fail only one to three quantities, and 31.6 % fail nine or more. The failures live in **low-biomass** forest — conjunctive pass runs 0.2–0.8 % across the four lowest biomass deciles against 14.6 % in the highest — and not in the tall stands the previous record's roster fix addressed.
- **Biomass is the one large error the original model's own noise does not excuse.** Across the 28,516 cells where LPJmL-FIT reproduces itself to within 10 %, so the signal is there to be learned, the emulator's median biomass error is 1.77× the tolerance and only 32.2 % of cells pass. Rooting depth is 1.11× and is the noisiest quantity the original model has (53 % of cells above the 10 % floor, median two-seed spread 22 %); the height tail sits at 0.60×, its median cell comfortably inside its band.
- **`k_root` is exactly constant** — zero error, zero two-seed spread, zero oracle gain in every arm — so the conjunctive test is effectively over **19** quantities, not 22. It was already known to sit at 1.000; what is new is that it contributes nothing to the statistic and must be disclosed whenever the 22 is quoted.

### Fixed

- **Four candidate cheap repairs of the height heads, all measured and all rejected**, which closes post-hoc repair as a route. The tail is unbiased (median predicted/true 0.9991), so there is no bias to remove; its log–log regression slope on the truth is 0.993, so there is no regression-to-the-mean shrinkage to undo; a one-parameter log-space spread-restoring map, fitted per fold on the remaining folds, makes the band test **worse**, 68.5 % → 67.8 %; and optimally fusing the tail with the biomass head cuts its error by 0.5 %, because the two errors are 78 % correlated. Any further gain needs different features or a different learner, not a recalibration.
