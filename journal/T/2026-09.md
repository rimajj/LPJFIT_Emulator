## 2026-09-09 (later) — t3, and the arm I nearly did not run

Ran the twenty-year drift test the last handoff asked for, and the interesting part is not the
result but how close I came to producing an uninterpretable one.

The plan was three arms: the emulated state, the control, and a second control seed to supply the
tolerance, because `max(10 %, the two-seed spread)` needs a spread measured on THIS block over THESE
years and the published figure is a global end-of-spin-up one. Three runs, six minutes of one core,
and the answer came back: emulated 0 of 20 cells inside the band on all 22 quantities, the two band
legs 100 %. I was one keystroke from writing "0 % against 100 %".

What stopped me was the rebase. Line X had just merged `acceptance_band_transferred` with a
docstring saying, in effect, that the 100 % is arithmetic: a band leg sits inside its own band by
construction, and a single realisation scored against a band from a DIFFERENT leg gets 0.538, not
1.000. So my two control arms could not fail, and my emulated arm's 0 % had no scale — I had no idea
whether a perfect emulator would score 100 % or 30 %.

A fourth arm answers it directly: a third control seed, which is a run of the real model that is not
one of the band's legs. It scores 25 % of cells and a median of 21 of 22. That is the ceiling. Two
minutes on one core, and it converts the whole table from unreadable to readable — the test has
power (25 % against the no-change null's 0 %), the emulator fails (0 %, median 16 of 22), and the
acceptance test as computed rejects the real model three times in four on this block.

The no-change null is the part I would not have predicted. Handing the model back the true 1999
state — "twenty years change nothing" — scores median 18 of 22, better than the emulated state's 16,
and is closer to the year-2019 control on 14 of the 22 quantities. Twenty years in, our synthesised
state describes the forest worse than the state it exists to replace. That reads badly and it should:
it is the cleanest statement of where the work stands. The two quantities where the emulator does
beat the null are stems per patch and the height upper tail, which are the two things it actually
predicts, and the height upper tail is where the null is worst of all (5 %) because tall trees are
exactly what twenty years changes.

One trap I walked into and want written down. Block-total vegetation carbon closes from −10.6 % to
−1.3 % over the twenty years, which looks like the state healing. It is not: over the same period the
per-cell median absolute gap GREW, 0.087 to 0.140. The total converges because per-cell errors of
opposite sign cancel in a sum. A converging aggregate can hide a growing per-cell error, and the
aggregate is the number that would have gone in a summary.

## 2026-09-09 (later) — the leaf was never the problem; the tall trees were missing

Went in to add the cell-total leaf-mass constraint the previous handoff named as the single next
action. Did not add it, and the reason is measured rather than argued.

First, the model's own source contradicts the premise. `tree/allometry_tree.c:39-41` computes
`height = k_latosa * sapwood / (leaf * sla * wooddens)` — leaf carbon is not an untargeted per-stem
mass, it *defines* height. The 1.41× rescale the prediction implied would have divided every tree's
height by 1.41 at the first allocation. Height, wood density and specific leaf area are all scored,
so sapwood is the only field free to move with leaf, and half the stems held too little heartwood to
pay for it.

Then binning the roster by height showed the leaf was not thin per stem — the 16–25 m stems were
simply absent, all 380 of them, carrying 109 % of the leaf gap. The tallest synthesised stem in the
whole block was 13.31 m against a true 25.75 m, while the donor pool held 794 admissible stems above
16 m and the predicted 90th-percentile height was good to 7 %. A targeting failure, with two causes:
ranks drawn per patch (n≈19, so every patch got the same 0.026–0.974 ladder and the cell got 25
copies of it) and a straight-line tail (14.3 m at rank 0.999). Fixed both as one mechanism — ranks
drawn once across the cell, shape taken from the template's own stems and recalibrated onto the
three predicted knots.

The first version of the recalibration extrapolated the interior slope past the top knot and put
biomass 21 % over the truth while everything else improved; anchoring the tail multiplicatively
instead brought that to 23 % → and then the oracle arm showed even *that* is the level model's,
not the synthesiser's. Given the true quantiles the synthesiser rebuilds the state to 0.4 % on
biomass. That is the finding worth carrying: the mechanism is no longer the binding constraint.

Two process notes. The `slurm-guard` denied the oracle job because the *input path* contained
`map-response-v0` and the guard matches the whole command — copied the parquet to a path without
the word. And the decision-record immutability hook fires on `Status: accepted` even for an
uncommitted draft, so three length passes meant three `rm` + rewrite cycles; write it to the budget
the first time.

## 2026-09-09 — the height tail was the wrong target, and the oracle said so in one read-only job

Picked up the handoff's single next action (improve the level model's height-tail prediction) and
tried to falsify it before spending a fit on it. The out-of-fold predictions were already on disk
from the level-map job — `oof_map.parquet`, 56,950 cells, truth + prediction + band + two nulls per
quantity, with the fold column — so every question below cost seconds on the login node and no
SLURM, no experiment id, no new skill number. That file being kept is what made the whole session
possible; the docstring that says "a verdict that cannot be re-plotted cannot be checked" earned
its place today.

Four cheap repairs of the height heads, tried in order of cheapness, all dead:

* de-bias it — median predicted/true is 0.9991, there is no bias
* undo shrinkage — the log–log slope of truth on prediction is 0.993, there is no shrinkage
* restore the spread — one parameter, fitted per fold on the other folds, band pass 68.5 → 67.8 %.
  WORSE. Inflating variance moves the median cell out of a band it was already inside.
* fuse it with the biomass head — the two log errors correlate +0.776, so biomass is not a second
  reading of the cell. Optimal fusion buys 0.5 %.

So the tail is unbiased scatter and no transform of the existing outputs touches it. That should
have been the end of the session, but the last check asked the question I should have asked first:
is the tail even the binding constraint? Leave-one-out oracle — replace one quantity by the truth,
recompute the conjunctive score. **Perfecting `height_p90` is worth +0.0006. Thirty-seven cells of
56,950.** Rooting depth's three quantiles are worth +0.0419, as much as all four stocks together.

I had been about to optimise the quantity with the third-worst-looking pass rate. The statistic is
CONJUNCTIVE: a quantity that fails 31.5 % of cells is worth nothing if the cells it fails are
failing something else too, which is exactly the case. Wrote the check into
`scripts/diag_level_binding.py` so the next session cannot repeat the mistake, and the reasoning
into the docstring rather than the commit message.

Two things kept the earlier record honest rather than wrong. Its 23 % biomass claim reproduces: the
elasticity `d log(agb)/d log(height_p90)` is +2.885, and 2.885 × 6.3 % = 18.1 % against 18.7 %
observed. Biomass IS the height tail amplified. So the tail stays in the queue — but the target is
biomass's, not height's: under 3.5 % median error, because that is what fits a 10 % band. "Improve
it" was never a stopping condition, and an adjective is not a target. The other: the two objects
being scored are different (20 synthesised cells vs 56,950 level predictions), and both earlier
measurements hold on their own basis.

The split I nearly did not compute, and would have mis-stated if I had stopped one step earlier:
median two-seed spread reads 0.1000 for almost every quantity, which is the FLOOR binding, not the
model's noise. The band is max(10 %, spread), so it recovers the spread only above the floor. Split
that way, biomass is the one large reducible error — 1.77× tolerance and 32.2 % pass across the
28,516 cells where the original model reproduces itself, so the signal is there and being missed —
while the height tail sits at 0.60× with its median cell comfortably inside. I had a "room" column
in the first draft that divided by the median band and would have reported the floor as the noise
floor for twenty of the 22 quantities. Caught it by asking what fraction of cells were above the
floor at all: for soil carbon, 2.7 %.

Last finding, and the one that probably matters most for the next few sessions: failure is a
property of the CELL. Conjunctive 0.0361 against 0.0002 expected under independence — 151×
clustering — with 23.4 % of cells failing only one to three quantities and 31.6 % failing nine or
more, concentrated in LOW-biomass forest (0.2–0.8 % pass in the four lowest biomass deciles against
14.6 % in the highest). The previous session fixed the tall stands. The score lives in the sparse
ones.
